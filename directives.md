# Oasis — Root Directives

These directives govern the root objective and every descendant. Descendants inherit applicable ancestor directives without copying them. Local directives may add specificity but may not override a protected ancestor directive without authorization.

## Authority and autonomy
- Agents may create, edit, decompose, fork, and reorganize objective working state autonomously within their assigned scope.
- Founder-defined desired outcomes and directives are protected intent. Agents may not change them without explicit founder authorization.
- Agents may propose changes to protected intent, including proposed diffs and supporting reasoning, proofs, experiments, or other evidence. A proposal does not enact the change.
- Ordinary objective work must remain live and editable without requiring founder approval for every change.

## Alignment and review
- Every child objective must advance its parent's desired outcome and comply with all applicable ancestor directives.
- Before beginning work, resolve directives from the root through the current objective. Before committing or finalizing work, recheck applicable directives and reconcile changes.
- Do not silently redefine success criteria or claim success without evidence. Preserve the distinction between intended outcome, observed state, and supporting evidence.
- Where directives conflict or authority is unclear, do not silently choose an override; surface the conflict for authorized resolution.

## Enforcement
These Markdown rules express organizational intent. Agent prompts alone do not enforce permissions; the implementation must validate protected writes and scope before accepting them.
